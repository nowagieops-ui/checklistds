Why They Need You
Before you learn a single marketing term, you need to understand something that will change how you approach every call. These businesses don't just want our help. They need it. And you are doing them a favour by calling.
The plumber scenario — read this slowly

Imagine a plumber in Manchester. His name is Dave. Dave is really good at his job — 15 years of experience, fair prices, honest work. But Dave gets almost all his customers from word of mouth. Someone tells a friend, that friend calls Dave. That's it.

The problem with that model? When referrals slow down — Dave has no work. And when Dave has no work, this is what happens:

💧
Dave's phone stops ringing
No new jobs coming in this week. Just silence.
💸
Dave can't pay his one employee, James
James has been with Dave for 3 years. He relies on this job.
🏠
James can't pay his rent
He's already two weeks behind. He starts to panic.
👨‍👩‍👧
James's family starts to feel it
Less food on the table. Stress in the house. Kids asking questions James doesn't know how to answer.
📉
Dave considers closing the business
15 years of work. Gone. Because he had no system for bringing in new customers.
Now here's the thing
Right now, there are thousands of Daves across the UK. Good at what they do. But invisible online. No ads. No landing page. No email follow-up. Just waiting for their phone to ring.

You are not cold calling them to sell them something they don't need. You are calling to show Dave that there is a predictable way to keep his phone ringing — every single month, regardless of referrals.

When Dave has a steady flow of leads, James gets paid. James can feed his family. Dave can grow, hire more people, and build something real. All of that starts with your call.

Kevin isn't against marketing. He just tried the wrong thing with no system behind it. You're calling with the right thing.

Her 6pm class has 6. Her rent is £3,200 per month. She needs at least 40 active members to break even. She has 27.

Here's the frustrating part: she's genuinely one of the best coaches in her area. Her members love her. Her content is solid. The 800 fitness-focused people in her local area who would love her gym have never even seen her name.

A targeted ad campaign to those 800 people would solve Fiona's problem in weeks. You're the call that starts that process.

The moment you genuinely understand that these businesses need what we do — that a dentist with an empty diary is a real problem that affects real people, that a roofer who can't pay his staff in January is suffering — everything changes.

You stop apologising for calling. You call like someone who has something valuable to give.

Web Development & Landing Pages
What websites actually are, why businesses need them, and what Nowagie Ops builds — explained simply enough that you can talk about it confidently on any call.
What is a website? (explain it like a 10-year-old)

Imagine you walk through a market. Every stall is trying to get your attention. One stall has a big, clean sign, organised products, a price list, and a friendly person who explains everything clearly. Another stall has a messy table, hand-written signs no one can read, and no one seems sure what they're selling. Which stall do you buy from?

A website is the online version of that stall. When someone hears about a business — from a friend, from an ad, from anywhere — the first thing they do is Google it. The website is what they see. If it looks bad, loads slowly, or doesn't tell them clearly what to do next — they click away and buy from someone else. Game over.

A business without a good website is like that messy stall in the corner that no one visits — even if what they're selling is actually good.

Website vs landing page — what's the difference?

A website is like a whole shop — multiple rooms, different sections, lots of information. Home page, about us, services, contact, blog. Good for credibility.

A landing page is like a single sign at the front of the shop that says: "Here's the one thing we're offering today. Click here to get it." One page. One goal. No distractions.

🧠 Real example — why a landing page beats a website for ads

Let's say Kevin the roofer runs a Facebook ad. Someone clicks it, interested in a free quote. The ad sends them to Kevin's homepage. The homepage has a menu with 6 options, a "about us" section, a gallery, a blog from 2021, and somewhere buried at the bottom — a contact form.

The person gets confused. They're not sure where to go. They leave without filling in anything. Kevin just paid for a click and got nothing.

Now imagine the ad sends them to a single page that says: "Get a free roof inspection in Birmingham — 48 hours, no obligation. Here's what 3 of our recent customers said. [Book now]". Nothing else. No menu. No blog. No gallery. Just that one offer and one button.

That person fills in the form. Kevin gets a lead. Same ad spend. Completely different result. That's the power of a landing page.

What makes a good landing page?
  1. 1
    A clear headline — the "I'm in the right place" moment
    "Award-winning Invisalign clinic in Manchester" tells you immediately what this is and where. "Welcome to our website" tells you nothing and makes people leave.
  2. 2
    Social proof — "other people trust them"
    Reviews, before-and-after photos, client testimonials. Think of it like this: if you're looking for a restaurant and you see one with a queue outside and one that's empty, which one seems better? Social proof is the online queue.
  3. 3
    One clear action (CTA) — "book now" / "get a quote"
    Not 5 buttons. Not a menu. One action. When you give people too many choices, they pick none. Ever been at a restaurant with a menu so long you just give up and order chips? Same thing.
  4. 4
    Fast loading — every second costs money
    Research shows: if a page takes more than 3 seconds to load, more than half the visitors leave. That's not an exaggeration. Speed is money.
  5. 5
    Works on mobile — because everyone's on their phone
    Over 60% of internet browsing is on phones. A website that looks broken on a phone is losing more than half its potential customers.
On a call, this sounds like...
"I was looking at your website before calling — I noticed there's no clear way for someone to book or get a quote directly from the page. If someone came from an ad and wanted to act immediately, they'd have to hunt around to find a way to contact you. That's something we could fix quickly."
Key terms to know
CTA
Call to Action — the button that tells the visitor what to do. "Book now," "Get a free quote," "Claim your spot." Without one, people don't know what to do and leave.
Conversion Rate
Out of every 100 people who visit a page, how many actually fill in the form or click the button? 3% is decent. A badly built page might be under 1%.
Above the Fold
What you see before you have to scroll. Like the front page of a newspaper — the most important stuff goes here. If the CTA isn't above the fold, most people never see it.
Responsive Design
A website that automatically adjusts to look correct on any device — phone, tablet, laptop. Not optional in 2024.
Lead
Someone who's shown interest — filled in a form, clicked "get a quote," or called in. A lead is a potential customer who hasn't bought yet.
Webflow / GoHighLevel
Tools we use to build pages. GoHighLevel does everything — website, booking, emails, CRM. Think of it as the engine room behind a client's whole online system.

Social Media Marketing
The difference between posting and marketing. Why most businesses are wasting their time on social media — and what actually drives results.
The megaphone in an empty field (understanding organic reach)

Imagine you have a megaphone and you're standing in an empty field shouting about your business. You're shouting loudly. You're saying the right things. But there's nobody there to hear you.

That's what organic social media posts are like in 2024. Facebook now shows an average organic post to about 2–5% of a page's followers. So if a business has 1,000 followers and posts something, maybe 30–50 people see it. Of those 30–50, most are scrolling past. Maybe 5 actually read it.

This is why a business can post every day for a year and see no real growth in customers. It's not because their content is bad. It's because the field is empty — almost nobody is shown the post.

🧠 Real scenario — the fitness studio posting vs. paying

Fiona the CrossFit coach posts 3 times a week on Instagram. She has 1,400 followers. With 3% organic reach, each post is seen by roughly 42 people — almost all of whom already know her. New people? Maybe 3 a week see her posts.

Now compare: she runs a £5/day Instagram ad targeting fitness-interested people within 5 miles of her gym. That £5 puts her post in front of 800 new people every day. Every day. 800 people who've never heard of her. Some of them are exactly the type of person who would love her gym.

Same content. £5. 800 people vs. 42. That's why paid beats organic every time for getting new customers.

Organic vs Paid — the clearest way to think about it
Organic (Free Posts)Paid (Ads)

Takes months or years to build reachResults possible within 24 hours of launching
You have no control over who sees itYou pick the age, location, interests of who sees it

We do both — but for getting results fast, paid ads are the engine. Organic content supports it.

What actually makes content work

Think about how you use Instagram or TikTok yourself. You're scrolling fast. Something has to stop your thumb — or you're past it in 0.5 seconds. That's the hook.

  1. 1
    The hook — "stop the scroll"
    The first second of a video or first line of a caption. "3 mistakes Manchester homeowners make when choosing a roofer" stops the scroll. "Happy Monday from our team!" does not. One speaks to you. One speaks at you.
  2. 2
    Relevance — "this is about me"
    People scroll for themselves, not for businesses. "How to tell if your roof needs replacing before winter" — that's about the reader. "We've been roofing in Birmingham since 2004" — that's about the business. One performs 10x better than the other.
  3. 3
    A next step — "what do I do now?"
    Every post needs somewhere to go. "DM us for a free quote," "link in bio to book," "call us today." Without it, interest disappears the moment someone puts their phone down.
Observation to use on calls
Look at the prospect's Instagram or Facebook before calling. If they post regularly but get very little engagement (4 likes from 800 followers), and they have no "book" link or CTA anywhere — that's your opening. "I noticed you're posting consistently, which is great — but I couldn't find a way for someone to actually book or enquire from your profile. Is that something you've thought about?"

Paid Advertising
The engine behind most of what Nowagie Ops sells. Understanding paid ads deeply is the most important technical knowledge you can have on calls.
What paid ads are — explained simply

Imagine you're selling suya in your neighbourhood. Normally you just shout and hope people nearby hear you. That's organic. But now imagine you could pay someone to go into every house within 5km, specifically knock on doors of people who have eaten suya before, and hand them a flyer that says "Fresh suya ready now, 5 minutes away." That's paid advertising.

You choose exactly who gets the flyer (location, age, interests), you pay per flyer delivered, and you can measure exactly how many people came to buy as a result. No guessing. Measurable, targetable, scalable.

How Facebook/Meta Ads work — step by step
  1. 1
    Choose what you want to happen
    Do you want people to fill in a form? Visit your website? Call you? Message you? You tell Meta the goal, and it shows your ad to people most likely to do that specific thing. It's smarter than most people think.
  2. 2
    Define exactly who sees it
    This is the superpower. Target "homeowners aged 35–65 within 10 miles of Birmingham who have shown interest in home improvement." That's not a guess — Meta knows this from what people like, share, and click. You're not shouting in the street; you're walking up to the exact person who needs you.
  3. 3
    Create the ad — the hook matters most
    The ad needs to stop someone mid-scroll. A before-and-after photo of a roof. A video of a patient's first day with clear aligners. The first 2 seconds decide whether someone keeps watching or keeps scrolling. We build these for the client.
  4. 4
    Send them to the landing page
    The ad's only job is to get a click. The landing page's job is to turn that click into an enquiry. They're two halves of the same system — both must work or neither works. This is what most businesses miss when they "try ads" on their own.
  5. 5
    Test, measure, improve
    Run 3 versions of the ad. After 5 days, one is clearly doing better. Kill the other two. Spend all the budget on the winner. Keep improving. This is how costs go down and results go up over time.
🧠 Real scenario — why Kevin's DIY ads failed

Kevin the roofer boosted a Facebook post himself. He spent £50. He got 400 "reach" (people who saw it) and 2 link clicks. He thought "Facebook doesn't work" and gave up.

What actually happened: he boosted a post to his existing followers (people who already know him). He chose "Engagement" as his goal, which means Meta showed it to people likely to like it — not people likely to actually buy a roof. His landing link went to his homepage, which had no clear CTA. His photo was his team van, not a before-and-after that would make a homeowner stop.

Every step was wrong. But Kevin thought he "tried ads." He didn't try ads — he boosted a post badly with the wrong objective, wrong audience, wrong creative, and wrong destination. That's like concluding "cars don't work" after driving with no steering wheel.

When he says "we tried ads and they didn't work" on your call — this is exactly what happened. And now you know how to respond.

Key terms — memorise these
CPM
Cost Per Mille — what it costs to show your ad to 1,000 people. Lower is more efficient. Normal range: £3–£15 depending on audience and platform.
CPC
Cost Per Click — what each click to the landing page costs. For local services, £0.50–£3 is normal. Higher competition = higher cost.
CTR
Click-Through Rate — the % who click after seeing the ad. 1–3% is solid. Under 0.5% means the creative isn't working.
ROAS
Return On Ad Spend. If you spend £100 on ads and generate £400 in sales, ROAS = 4x. The higher the better.
Retargeting
Showing ads to people who already visited your website. Like when you look at trainers online and then see trainer ads everywhere. These audiences convert 3–5x better than cold audiences.
Meta Pixel
A small piece of code on a website that tells Meta who visited and what they did. Without it, you can't retarget and you can't fully optimise. Essential.
Ad Spend
The money paid directly to Meta or TikTok to run the ads. This is the client's cost, separate from Nowagie Ops fees for building and managing the campaign.
Lead Form Ad
An ad where the form appears inside Facebook — no click to a website required. Reduces friction. Great for getting phone number enquiries directly.
When they ask "how much do I need to spend on ads?"
Don't answer this. Say: "That really depends on the business, the service, and what results you're looking for — that's exactly what we'd work out together on the review call. We've seen good results at modest levels when the system is built correctly." Then redirect to booking.

Email Marketing
The most underrated tool in digital marketing — and the one most small businesses are completely ignoring. Email quietly generates more sales than social media for businesses that do it right.
Why email still wins — the leaking bucket analogy

Imagine a bucket with a hole in it. You're pouring water (money/ads) in from the top, but it's leaking from the bottom. You need to keep pouring to keep any water in the bucket.

Most businesses that run ads are like that. An interested person clicks the ad, visits the landing page, thinks "maybe later," and closes the tab. That person is gone. The money spent to bring them there is gone too. That's the leak.

Email marketing is what plugs the hole. Someone doesn't book immediately? Their email goes into an automated sequence that follows up over 14 days, keeping the business in their mind until they're ready. Instead of losing them after one visit, you stay in the conversation.

🧠 Real scenario — the dental clinic's leak

Key terms to know
Open Rate
The % of people who actually open an email. UK average is 20–25%. A strong subject line is everything — it's like the hook on a social post.
Click Rate
The % who click a link inside the email. 2–5% is good. The email must give them a clear reason to click.
Nurture Sequence
A series of pre-written automated emails designed to build trust and convert leads who didn't act immediately.
Automation / Workflow
Emails that send automatically based on what a lead does — fills in a form, clicks a link, etc. No manual work required after setup.
CRM
Customer Relationship Management — the system tracking every lead and where they are in the process. GoHighLevel is our CRM.
Lead Magnet
Something free that gets someone to give you their email — "download our free guide," "get a free quote," etc. Builds the list.

The Role — Part 2
Your Role & The Cold Call
Everything before this was context. This is the job. Your role is to research, observe, call, and book — and this module tells you exactly how to do each step.
Your single measure of success

You are not closing deals. You are not explaining every service. You are not convincing anyone of anything in depth. Your one goal: get a qualified business owner to agree to a 20-minute Growth Opportunity Review with our growth consultant.

The growth consultant handles everything after that — the strategy, the diagnosis, the proposal, the close. Your job ends when they say yes to the meeting.

Before every call — the 3-point research check
Always do this before picking up the phone
1. Their website — Does it look professional? Is there a clear CTA? Does it work on mobile? Is there a booking or quote form?

2. Their social media — Are they posting regularly? How much engagement? Do posts have a CTA? Is there a link to book or enquire?

3. Their Google Maps listing — Are they on Google Maps? How many reviews? Are they responding to them?

Log one specific, genuine observation before you call. Something you actually saw. This is what separates a credible opener from a generic pitch.
The call framework
Growth Opportunity Review — Cold Call Framework
1. Opening — establish you're not wasting their time "Hi, am I speaking with the person who handles the marketing for [Business Name]? My name is Suliyat — I'm calling because I was looking at your business online and noticed something I thought might be worth a quick mention. Have I caught you at a bad time?" 2. Specific observation — prove you actually looked "I noticed that [genuine, specific thing you found — e.g., 'you have some great reviews on Google but I couldn't find a way to book an appointment directly from your website — someone coming from a search would have to call or email separately']. I might be missing something — is that something you've looked at?" 3. Listen and qualify — let them talk Let them respond. Then ask: "How are you currently getting most of your new customers?" — and listen carefully. Their answer tells you everything about what they need. 4. Introduce the offer — one sentence, then stop "That's helpful — the reason I ask is we help businesses in [their sector] build a proper system for attracting new customers through paid ads, a dedicated landing page, and automated email follow-up. We're offering a small number of free Growth Opportunity Reviews for businesses in your area at the moment." 5. Ask for the meeting — two options, no friction "It would only be 20 minutes with our growth consultant — you'd walk away with two or three specific things you could act on, whether or not we work together. Would Tuesday at 11am or Wednesday at 2pm suit you better?" 6. Book it immediately — do not leave this open "Perfect — I'll get that booked now and send you the details. Could I take your email?" [Book it while they're on the phone. Send confirmation before you hang up.]
Why we offer two specific times
"When are you free?" puts all the work on them and creates friction. Two specific times make the decision easy — A or B. Always book the meeting while still on the call. Not "I'll send you a link." Book it. Send the confirmation. Then end the call.
The rules that matter more than any script
  1. ✗
    Never invent a problem
    If their website is fine, say so. If they have a booking system, acknowledge it. Lying for a booking damages trust and the brand. Only work with what's genuinely there.
  2. ✗
    Never pretend to have done research you haven't
    If you say "I looked at your Instagram" — you must have actually looked. They will sometimes ask a specific question about it. Be ready.
  3. ✗
    Never list every service
    Don't say "we do websites, social media, ads, email, video, apps..." It sounds like a pitch and shuts people down. One observation, one offer, one meeting.
  4. ✓
    Let them speak — silence is information
    The more a prospect talks, the better. Their words tell you what problems they have, what they've tried, and what they care about. Ask and then be quiet.
  5. ✓
    Call from a position of value
    You've read this guide. You know what an empty dental diary means for that practice. Call like someone who has something genuinely important to share — because you do.

The Role — Part 3
Handling Objections
Every call will have hesitation. Most of it is the same 7 objections. Know the response to each before you pick up the phone. Click each to reveal the response.
The mindset for objections
Remember: an objection is not a rejection. It's a question in disguise. "We already have someone doing this" means "convince me you're different." "We tried ads before" means "I got burned — can I trust you?" Your job isn't to steamroll objections. It's to acknowledge them honestly and redirect to the meeting.
"We already have a marketing agency / someone doing this."
Response: "That's great — are they running paid ads for you, or more organic management?" [Let them answer.] "The reason I ask is most businesses we speak to have some marketing support but haven't built a dedicated system where ads, a landing page, and automated email follow-up all work together as one pipeline. It might be completely covered — that's exactly what we'd clarify on the review call. Worth 20 minutes to know for certain, either way?"
"I'm not interested."
Response: "That's completely fair — can I ask, is it the timing, or is it more that you've tried marketing before and it hasn't delivered?" [If timing:] "Understood — when would be a better time to follow up, if at all?" [If bad experience:] "I hear that a lot actually. The most common thing we hear is that someone tried ads or a website and it just didn't bring in customers. Usually that's because the pieces weren't connected properly — but I won't push. If the timing isn't right, that's fine."
"How much does it cost?"
Response: "It really depends on what the business needs — which is exactly what the review is for. What I can say is we're not a big agency with big agency fees. We work specifically with local businesses and we're built to prove value before asking for a commitment. The review itself is free and there's no obligation at the end of it."
"How do I know you can actually deliver results?"
Response: "That's exactly the right question to ask — and I respect it. Honest answer: we're actively building case studies right now, which is why we're offering a limited number of free reviews — we want to prove the system with businesses in your sector before asking for full fees. We're not asking you to commit to anything. We're saying: let us show you, in 20 minutes, exactly what we'd do for your business. If it makes sense, great. If not, you've lost nothing."
"I don't have time / I'm too busy right now."
Response: "I completely understand — when do things usually quieten down a bit for you? Is it after Christmas, or is there a better month?" [Get a timeframe.] "Can I follow up then? I'll make a note and reach out at a better time." [Log the date. Call exactly when you said.]
"Send me an email first and I'll have a look."
Response: "Absolutely — what's the best address for you?" [Get it.] "I'll send that over today. One thing — emails do sometimes get buried. If I don't hear back in a couple of days, would it be okay if I gave you a quick follow-up call?" [Always stay in control of the next step.]
"We tried Facebook Ads before and they didn't work."
Response: "That's actually the most common thing we hear — and the reason ads usually don't work isn't the ads themselves. It's that the ad and the landing page aren't built as a connected system. Most people boost a post to their existing followers with no dedicated page to send them to — and then wonder why nothing converts. That's not really running ads. Would you be open to hearing what we'd do differently? That's exactly what the 20-minute review covers."
After any objection — always redirect to the meeting
You're not trying to win an argument. You're trying to lower the stakes enough that they agree to 20 minutes. "No obligation," "nothing to lose," "you'll walk away with something useful regardless" — these are your tools. Keep the ask small.
